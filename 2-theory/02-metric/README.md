## The three questions, in dependency order

**1. Light travel time** ([OPN-009](../../traceability/claims/OPN-009.yaml)). What
accounts for light presently arriving from cosmological distances. Not addressed in the
position paper. Existing young-cosmos treatments should be **surveyed and priced**, not
reinvented — the literature has already refuted several of them, and rediscovering a
refuted option is the cheapest available mistake.

**2. Processes observed in transit**
([OPN-010](../../traceability/claims/OPN-010.yaml)). Sharper, less often addressed, and
where the programme is most exposed. Distant observations record processes with internal
duration, not merely arrived photons: $(1+z)$ supernova light-curve time dilation, the
SN 1987A ring light-echo geometry, quasar variability, binary pulsar spin-down. Any
account of light in transit makes *some* commitment about what these records are, and
that commitment is where a discriminator lives.

This also bears directly on [CRE-007](../../traceability/claims/CRE-007.yaml). A mature
tree is a configuration; a supernova light curve is a *process apparently completed*. The
paper's disclaimer that operational completeness does not entail deception is asserted
for the first case and has to be argued for the second.

**3. Quantitative redshift** ([OPN-004](../../traceability/claims/OPN-004.yaml)). A
created-geometry model must reproduce the observed distance-redshift relation across the
measured range. **The residuals are the opportunity:** such a model will not in general
reproduce the $(1+z)$ time-dilation scaling exactly, and the size and shape of the
mismatch is a candidate prediction that current supernova samples can already constrain.
See [discriminators.md](../../3-prediction/discriminators.md).
